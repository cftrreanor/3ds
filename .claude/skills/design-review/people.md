# The six people

Check every design against all six. For each one, ask: can this person do their job on this
screen in the time and attention they actually have?

## 1. Organizer at a laptop

Sets up and runs the event: a band booster, a choir parent, or a school staff member.
- **Device:** laptop or desktop at home, sometimes a tablet
- **Time:** an evening session of 30 to 60 minutes, spread across several weeks
- **Attention:** focused, but not an expert. They set up one or two events a year and forget how
  things work in between.
- **Needs:** to know what's done, what's left, and what happens when they publish. To not lose
  work. To trust what people will see.
- **Breaks for them:** hidden steps, unclear "is this saved?", jargon, a setting whose effect they
  can't preview

## 2. Volunteer parent on a phone outdoors

Signs up for a shift, then shows up on the day.
- **Device:** phone, often one-handed, bright sun, weak signal
- **Time:** two minutes, often from a link in a group text
- **Attention:** low, and distracted by kids
- **Needs:** what, when, and where at a glance. Sign up without making an account. Find their
  shift again on the day.
- **Breaks for them:** small tap targets, low contrast, long forms, anything that needs a
  password, text that only makes sense to the organizer

## 3. Band director between classes

Registers a band for a contest and gets their times.
- **Device:** a school laptop or a phone, between classes or on a bus
- **Time:** five minutes at a time, interrupted by a bell
- **Attention:** expert in their field, impatient with software
- **Needs:** the next step, the deadline, the times, and the parking. Proof it's handled.
- **Breaks for them:** forms that lose progress, information buried under marketing, unclear
  deadlines

## 4. Busy mom who is also PTO president

Runs several school events a year on top of a family and maybe a job.
- **Device:** phone most of the time, laptop late at night
- **Time:** short bursts while waiting in the car line, and a longer session after the kids are in
  bed
- **Attention:** split many ways, often tired
- **Needs:** to pick up exactly where she left off. Hand jobs to other parents. Reuse last year's
  event. Know nothing is falling through the cracks.
- **Breaks for them:** setup that has to be done in one sitting, no "where was I?", too many
  decisions up front, nothing reusable

## 5. Teacher with a 30-minute lunch

Sets up conferences, a class party, or a campus visit, and checks bookings.
- **Device:** classroom computer or phone
- **Time:** whatever's left of a 30-minute lunch, so 10 to 15 minutes. Can't come back until
  after school.
- **Attention:** focused but rushed. Students may walk in at any moment.
- **Needs:** finish a task completely in one short window. Defaults that just work. A printable
  list.
- **Breaks for them:** long setups, steps that require information they don't have on hand,
  anything that times out or gets lost mid-way

## 6. Front office administrator

Runs the front desk while parents come into the building, and keeps an eye on schedules, kids,
teachers, and many other things.
- **Device:** a desktop at the front desk, sometimes a tablet, often a shared login
- **Time:** seconds at a time, constantly interrupted by people at the counter and phone calls
- **Attention:** fragmented. They look away and come back many times a minute.
- **Needs:** see who's expected, who's here, and who's late in one glance. Check someone in with
  one tap. Pick up exactly where they were after an interruption. Be sure about the safety
  details: who is allowed to pick up which child, and visitor IDs.
- **Breaks for them:** anything that resets or scrolls away when they look up, small text,
  multi-step check-ins, unclear status, alerts that are easy to miss or that cry wolf
