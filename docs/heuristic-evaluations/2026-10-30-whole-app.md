## Summary
Field Command already looks calmer and more trustworthy than most school software, and individual pages are well made. The gap is between pages: the same job (publish, show status, name a page, show a number, delete) is done a different way on almost every page, so experts relearn the app as they move through it and red stops meaning danger. The biggest risks are on event day (a Today button that opens the wrong page for two event types, and emergency messages that can sit below routine ones); both are small fixes. Root cause: the shared kit (`src/components/ui.tsx`) has 7 building blocks and the app needs about 20.

**Issues by severity:** 4 catastrophe: 0 · 3 major: 5 · 2 minor: 22 · 1 cosmetic: 8

Severity is the average of three ratings (A/B/C). "Found by" is how many reviewers raised it on their own.

## Starting fresh: what would make it iconic

**One frame for every event.** Every event lives in the same shell: a slim bar with the event name, date, and a Draft / Live / Today / Over status, then one row of tabs (Overview · Sign-ups · Schedule · Volunteers · Messages · Day · Settings). Each event type turns tabs on or off and renames the noun. Titles, buttons, checklist steps, and the day bar all read from one list, so they can never drift again. _Fixes P1, P3, P5, P6, P8 at the root._

**Plan, Live, and Wrap-up.** The event changes shape with the calendar. Plan shows the checklist and setup. Live turns into Field mode: high contrast, big targets, today's jobs and messages first, offline-safe, nothing else in the way. Wrap-up shows thank-yous, volunteer hours, what ran late, and "Copy for next year". _No competitor designs for the parking lot at noon. This is the signature.._

**One status language.** Five states only (Draft, Open, Live, Closed, Needs you), each with a fixed colour, shape, and word, used the same way in badges, tabs, emails, and the public page. One publish bar everywhere: blue turns on, neutral turns off, red only deletes. _Recognizable from across the gym, the way Linear's issue states are at 12px._

**Your pass.** Every parent, volunteer, and visitor gets one personal page per event: what they signed up for, when and where, directions, add to calendar, change or cancel, live messages, and on the day a big QR code for check-in. Every email points there. _Replaces three different "manage" links with one thing people learn once._

**Tell us about your event.** Replace the type picker and wizard with three plain questions (what's happening, when and where, what do you need from people). Build a working draft from the answers and open a live preview of the parent page: "Looks right? Publish." Plus templates and "Start from last year". _Five minutes from sign-up to a shareable link._

**A home that says what's next.** On sign-in: "Tonight: 2 expired invites · Tomorrow 7:00 AM: you lead Parking · Due Friday: publish finals", each a one-tap action. A ⌘K jump-to for experts. _The admin Follow-ups pattern, turned toward customers._

**The run sheet as the hero.** The director's vertical timeline (warm-up, ready, perform) is the most distinctive screen in the app. Make it the house component for every type, with a live "now" line: the volunteer's shift, the parent's conference slot, the choir's room rotation. The public page becomes a live scoreboard: now performing, up next. _The screenshot people share._

**An identity from the field.** Yard lines and hash marks as the brand graphic (dividers, progress bars), a custom set of about 40 icons (bus, music stand, gate, booth tent, ID badge) to replace emoji, a condensed scoreboard face for event names and big times, and one meaningful animation: the finalist reveal. Printouts get the same care, because clipboards will exist on day one anyway. _Ownable, and instantly reads "school events"._

## Deliberate trade-offs
- **Privacy rules add explanation.** Phone numbers only on event day, children's details deleted after 30 days, and families not seeing who booked all need extra words. They're core values and well explained; just fold the repeat paragraphs into one "ⓘ" line once someone has read them.
- **No passwords for parents and volunteers.** Email links and "remember this device" are right for this audience. P25 only asks to extend it to bookings and registrations.
- **Check-in locked until the day.** Prevents early check-ins. Keep it; just fix the message after the event (P20).
- **A denser admin area.** The admin CRM uses smaller controls on purpose, and DESIGN.md documents it.
- **Summary choices on Volunteer setup.** You asked for the daily-summary choices to appear on a Volunteer setup card as well as in Email settings. Two reviewers felt a personal setting on a shared page is easy to miss; worth watching in user tests, not changing now.

## Considered and set aside
- Count mismatches between the event page and setup pages ("4 bands" vs "3 bands", "412 signed up" in 19 spots): most likely the sample data. Worth a quick check with real data.
- Band name vs school name order on the contest-day board: a real but small difference; folded into the shared building blocks work.
- "The help text is too long" on setup pages: experts find it repetitive, first-timers find it helpful. Fold it after first read rather than cut it.

## Limits of this review
This is an expert review by an AI against established usability guidelines, not testing with real users. It finds likely problems quickly, but it can miss some and misjudge how much they matter. The app ran with sample data, so a few pages (the director's home, the choir room setup) couldn't be rendered and were judged from code. Next step: watch 3–5 real organizers set up an event and run check-in on a phone, and 3–5 parents sign up, focusing on the top priorities above.
