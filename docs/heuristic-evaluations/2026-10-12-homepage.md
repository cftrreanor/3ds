| | |
|---|---|
| **Product** | Field Command Events |
| **Task / scope** | A first-time visitor lands on the homepage, works out what this is and whether it's for them, and requests a pilot spot using the form at the bottom. |
| **Users** | Prospective hosts: PTO/PTA leaders, booster club volunteers, band and fine arts directors, school front offices. Often on a phone, skimming between other jobs, and new to the company. |

| **Date** | 2026-10-12 |

## Summary
The homepage has a clear, friendly headline. It's written in the language schools use, and it's unusually honest about privacy and about what the pilot asks of a school. The biggest risk is **trust**. A skeptical newcomer never sees the product, can't tell who is behind it, sees only a Gmail contact address, and finds no privacy policy or terms to forward to a principal or district. The second risk is the **request form**. A small slip, like a 7-digit phone number, produces an error that on a phone usually appears out of view. Errors arrive one at a time, so fixing them takes several tries.

**Issues by severity:** 4 catastrophe: 0 · 3 major: 2 · 2 minor: 7 · 1 cosmetic: 9
<sub>Severity is the average of the three evaluators' 0–4 ratings, rounded to the nearest whole number for these counts.</sub>

## Findings by heuristic

### 1. Visibility of system status
_Does the design clearly communicate its state? Is feedback presented quickly after user actions?_

**Working well**
- While the request sends, the button changes to "Sending…" and can't be pressed twice.
- After a request is sent, the form is replaced with "✓ Request sent" and says what happens next ("We'll be in touch within a few days").
- "Takes a minute. We'll reply within a few days." sets expectations before the visitor starts.

**Issues and recommendations**

| Issue | Where | Severity | Found by | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| **The thank-you message appears twice and the reading position is lost.** On success, the form becomes a "Request sent" panel *and* a pop-up toast shows the same thanks at the top of the screen. ### 2. Match between system and the real world
_Will the user be familiar with the terminology used in the design? Do the design's controls follow real-world conventions?_

**Working well**
- The headline "School events, without the clipboards." and phrases like "front office", "photo ID" and "returning directors" are the visitor's own words.
- The FAQ is phrased the way hosts actually ask: "Can I use this instead of a Google Form for parent check-in?"
- Band content uses real band language ("marching invitationals, solo & ensemble").

**Issues and recommendations**

| Issue | Where | Severity | Found by | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Internal role names and jargon.** "Zone", "Section Leads", "Volunteer Leads", "Station Coordinators" and "live order" are the product's terms. A PTO parent has to guess what they mean. Band directors recognise most of them. | Event-type cards, features, FAQ · `src/app/page.tsx:12`, `:66`, `:150` | 1.3 | 1/3 (A) | Use everyday words ("area of the fair", "the parent running each booth") or explain each term once, the first time it appears. |
| **What the product does for band contests is unclear.** Judging, scoring and recaps, classes and divisions, and entry fees aren't mentioned. ### 3. User control and freedom
_Does the design allow users to go back a step in the process? Are exit links easily discoverable? Can users easily cancel an action? Is Undo and Redo supported?_

**Working well**
- The whole pitch is on one page with jump links, so the browser Back button always works as expected.
- "Sign in" is always visible in the header for returning hosts.
- After the pilot: "Nothing renews or charges automatically". Visitors aren't locked into anything.
- Pressing submit twice by accident is treated as one request, not an error.

**Issues and recommendations**

| Issue | Where | Severity | Found by | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Visitors who aren't hosts have nowhere to go.** A parent, volunteer or visiting director who searches the company name to find *their* event lands here. The page is entirely a sales pitch to hosts. The only way forward is "Sign in", which suggests they need an account (they don't). | Header and hero · `src/app/page.tsx:232` | 1.3 | Raised during consolidation; rated by all three | Add a small line near the top: "Volunteering, registering or visiting? ### 4. Consistency and standards

- The violet pill-shaped button appears only in the header and hero, as the design guide (`docs/DESIGN.md`) intends.

### 5. Error prevention
_Does the design prevent slips by using helpful constraints? Does the design warn users before they perform risky actions?_

**Working well**
- Empty required fields are caught straight away by the browser ("Please fill out this field"), before anything is sent (`phone-form-empty-submit.png`).
- The fields bring up the right phone keyboard (email, phone) and support autofill for name, email, phone and organization.
- Event types are tick-boxes, not free text, so answers can't be misspelled.

**Issues and recommendations**

| Issue | Where | Severity | Found by | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The form's rules are hidden until you break them.** The phone number must be 10 digits, and at least one event type must be ticked. The form says neither. "Pick all that apply" even reads as optional. Phone is required with no reason given, which makes a cautious visitor worry about sales calls. | Request form · `src/app/pilot-form.tsx:39`, `:47`; checks in `src/app/pilot-actions.ts:39`, `:42` | 2.0 | 3/3 | Under Phone, add: "10 digits, with area code. We'll use it to set up a short call." Change the event-type hint to "Pick at least one". Check both before sending. |

### 6. Recognition rather than recall
_Does the design keep important information visible, so that users do not have to memorize it? Does the design offer help in-context?_

**Working well**
- Field labels stay visible while typing. The organization example ("e.g. Oak Elementary PTA") is extra, not a replacement for the label.
- Each checkbox has a large, full-row tap area, and the chosen state is clearly highlighted.
- Typed answers stay in the form after an error, so nothing has to be re-entered.

**Issues and recommendations**

| Issue | Where | Severity | Found by | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **"Optional" sits below the comment box.** Visitors read it only after the box, so some will hesitate over whether they must fill it in. Screen readers do announce it. | Request form · `src/app/pilot-form.tsx:61` | 1.0 | 1/3 (C) | Put it in the label: "What's the hardest part … today? (optional)". |

### 9. Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors
_Does the design use traditional error message visuals, like bold, red text? Does the design offer a solution that solves the error immediately?_

**Working well**
- Errors are in plain language, in a red box, and announced to screen readers.
- Everything the visitor typed is kept after an error.
- If something fails, or too many requests arrive at once, the message gives the support email as a fallback.

**Issues and recommendations**

| Issue | Where | Severity | Found by | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Errors are hard to find and come one at a time.** After a mistake, a single message appears above the *whole* form. **Working well**

- The pilot section's "What you get / What we ask / After the pilot" sets clear expectations about commitment.
- The support email is in the footer, and the FAQ is also published in a form search engines and AI assistants can read.

## Deliberate trade-offs
- **Spam protection without a CAPTCHA.** The hidden trap field and the minimum time on the page block bots without making visitors solve puzzles. That's the right call for older and less technical visitors. Only the wording of the rare false alarm needs fixing (heuristic 9).
- **A long FAQ.** Part of its job is to answer questions for search engines and AI assistants. Keep the content, but make it tap-to-expand on phones.
- **Collecting very little data.** Headcounts instead of student names, and automatic deletion after the event, limit some features on purpose. Evaluators saw this as the page's strongest selling point to schools. Make it *more* prominent, not less.
- **No published price yet.** This is understandable while pricing is set during the pilot. "Nothing charges automatically" and "no fee for anyone you invite" soften it, but a range would still help (heuristic 10).
- **Pill-shaped buttons only in the header and hero.** The design guide sanctions this to make the main action stand out, so it isn't a consistency problem.
- **Requiring a phone number.** It supports the promised short call, so keep it if it's needed, but say why next to the field (heuristic 5).

## Considered and set aside
- **12px of sideways overflow at 280px width.** Only very rare, very narrow phones are affected. 320px and up are fine.
- **The red "1 Issue" badge in the screenshots.** It's a developer tool that appears only when running the site locally, not on the real site.

## Limits of this review
This is an expert review by an AI against established usability guidelines, not testing with real users. It finds likely problems quickly, but it can miss some and misjudge how much they matter. The three "evaluators" were separate AI reviews with different points of view, not three different people. The site ran locally with placeholder settings, so live site banners and the real email delivery weren't seen, and the successful submission was checked from the code only. Next step: watch 3–5 real PTO leaders and band directors land on the homepage and try to request a pilot spot, focusing on the top priorities above. Ask especially whether they'd trust the company enough to sign up.
